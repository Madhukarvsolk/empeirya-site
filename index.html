I · Skills

The goal I nearly lost

Engineering, years one to four
What happened
Engineering gives you four years and a hundred reasons to spend them on something else: marks for their own sake, trends, whatever everyone around you is doing.
What I felt
Busy, but unsure it was leading anywhere. Being busy felt like progress. It isn't the same thing.
What I tried
I kept coming back to one question: what can I build that a real company would need?
4
What I learned
Never divert from the goal. Use the four years to build real industry skills. Everything else can wait; this can't.
What I'd do differently
Start building real things in the first year, not the last one.
If you're starting now
Every semester, ask yourself: what can I do today that I couldn't do six months ago?

What happened
Kiara arrived at 4:52 in the morning after nineteen hours of labour, much louder than anyone expected.
What I felt
Love, yes. And terror. The nurse showed me how to hold her head, then left the room. I didn't move for an hour.

You don't need to know everything on day one. You only need to show up for the next feed.

Told by voice · 12 min · on the night bus home 15
II · Eleven nos

The twelfth question

What happened
Our eleventh pitch ended in four minutes. The twelfth investor also said no — but first she asked who our customer really was. We argued about it in the lift, all the way down.
What I felt
Embarrassed, then strangely light. Someone had finally pointed at the actual hole in the boat.
What we tried
We wrote down every question we had failed to answer that year, and built the next version to answer them.
16
What I learned
A no is a free lesson. Collect enough of them and they become the plan.
What I'd do differently
Talk to customers before the deck, not after. The garage was comfortable; the market wasn't in it.
If you're starting now
Write down the question that ends each meeting. The one you can't answer yet is the work.

Filmed on a phone · 2 min · a QR code in the printed book 19
4 · The first winter

Dark at four o'clock

What happened
Month four. Dark when I left work, dark when I woke. I knew the barista's order better than she knew my name — because she didn't.
What I felt
Invisible. Not sad, exactly — unwitnessed. My funny stories had nobody who knew the people in them.
What I tried
One month of saying yes to everything: five-a-side, a terrible book club, a colleague's cousin's birthday. Two of those stuck.
20
What I learned
Home is not a place you left. It's a thing you learn to carry, and to build twice.
What I'd do differently
Call home on Sundays from the start. Guilt grows quietly in the gap between calls.
If you're leaving now
Pack less. Say yes for a month. Learn one local joke and tell it badly.

02 · How it works

Live it. Say it. The book writes itself.

  1. 1

    Capture it any way

    Talk, type, chat, film, photograph, or write by hand and scan the page — in the moment or years later. Any life, any age: a school term, a first job, a wedding year, a recovery.

  2. 2

    It asks like a biographer

    The AI is trained on the oldest shape of a good story — a person, a challenge, what it felt like, what it taught — and draws the feeling out without ever naming it. No judgement, no bias: your answer is always the final answer.

  3. 3

    It drafts five ways, you choose

    Your own words come back laid out in five creative structures, readable as PDFs. Add your thoughts, reshape it, go again — it reiterates until you say it's final. A student's book reads like a student's; a kid's looks like a kid's.

  2. 2

    Capture in the moment

    Talk to it at 3 a.m. Add photos and video, or scan a page you wrote by hand. Nothing to format.

  3. 3

    It asks what matters

    The AI notices what keeps coming up and asks the question a biographer would.

  4. 4

    It writes the page

    Your rambling words come back as clean, warm prose — your phrases kept, the lesson highlighted.

  5. 5

    You approve

    Read it, tweak a word, choose between the structures it drafted from your story. Nothing is printed that you didn't approve.

  6. 6

    Printed, shipped, alive

    When the season ends, the hardcover arrives. Videos live on as QR codes beside the story they belong to.
